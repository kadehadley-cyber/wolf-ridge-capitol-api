Your Treatment Day: What to Expect

You're scheduled for a regenerative therapy treatment. Here's how to prepare, what happens during your visit, and how to take care of yourself afterward.

Before your visit

  • Eat a normal meal and drink plenty of water — being well-hydrated helps, especially for IV treatments.
  • Take your regular medications unless your provider told you otherwise.
  • Avoid anti-inflammatory medicines (ibuprofen, naproxen) for the days your provider specifies — they can work against the treatment.
  • Wear comfortable clothing with easy access to the treatment area.
  • Arrange a ride home if you were told sedation or a nerve block is involved.

During your visit

  • Plan for roughly 45–90 minutes in the office.
  • Your provider will confirm your consent and answer last questions.
  • The treatment is delivered by injection or IV infusion, depending on your plan.
  • Mild pressure or discomfort at the site is normal and brief.

After your visit

  • Mild soreness, warmth, or fullness at the site for 24–72 hours is common.
  • Ice is fine for comfort; skip anti-inflammatories unless your provider approves them.
  • Ease back into activity as directed — most patients resume normal routines the next day.
  • Follow the specific aftercare sheet for your protocol.

Call us if…

You develop fever over 101°F, spreading redness, severe or worsening pain, or any symptom that concerns you. Call the office number above — we would always rather hear from you.

This guide is general education and does not replace the personalized instructions from your provider. Regenerative biologic therapies are not FDA-approved to treat, cure, or prevent any disease; individual results vary.
