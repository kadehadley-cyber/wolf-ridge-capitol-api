NEURO · COGNITIVE DECLINE
Cognitive decline & dementia support
NOVA-ELITE 2 cc intranasal via catheter · NOVA-MSC FLEX 1–2 cc deltoid IM
An adjunctive framework for mild cognitive impairment or diagnosed dementia. Intranasal delivery through the olfactory and trigeminal pathways bypasses the blood–brain barrier and may support reduction of neuroinflammation and neuronal repair signaling. It does not replace disease-specific medical management.

Patient selection
  • •Documented mild cognitive impairment or dementia diagnosis with baseline cognitive testing on file
  • •Caregiver present to consent where needed and report changes
  • •Medically stable, able to sit with the head tilted back for 10 minutes at a time
  • •Clear nasal passage: no active sinus infection, severe congestion, or recent nasal surgery
Exclusion
  • •Active sinus or respiratory infection, or recent nasal or sinus surgery
  • •Severe deviated septum without ENT clearance
  • •Pregnancy unless approved by the Medical Director
  • •Agitation or delirium that prevents safe positioning — reschedule rather than restrain
Administration & aftercare
SEQUENCE — FULL TECHNIQUE ON PAGE 08
1
Seat the patient, head tilted back 30–45°. Draw 2 cc into two syringes and attach the catheter per manufacturer instructions.
2
Left nostril: instill 1 cc over 10–20 seconds, hold the position 8–10 minutes, then wait 10 minutes.
3
Right nostril: repeat, holding another 8–10 minutes.
4
Administer 1–2 cc NOVA-MSC FLEX into the deltoid, then observe 5–10 minutes.
POST-PROCEDURE INSTRUCTIONS
  • •No nose blowing for at least 60 minutes
  • •Avoid bending forward for 20–30 minutes
  • •Hydrate normally; no alcohol the same day
  • •Mild throat drainage is expected and harmless
FOLLOW-UP
First follow-up at 2 weeks, second at 6 weeks. Repeat every 4–6 weeks by symptom severity, re-evaluating after 2–3 sessions.
OUTCOME MEASURES
MoCA OR MMSE CAREGIVER-REPORTED ADLs SLEEP QUALITY AGITATION FREQUENCY
Record the same instrument at baseline and at the 6-week visit. Caregiver observations are part of the record, not an aside.
RISKS & SAFETY
Sinus discomfort or pressure, transient headache, throat drainage, and epistaxis are the expected complaints. Monitor for sinus infection after the visit. Continue all standard dementia care, including prescribed medication and cognitive or behavioral support.
NO CURE CLAIM
Nothing in this protocol is offered as a treatment for Alzheimer's disease or any other dementia, and no claim of slowing, halting, or reversing cognitive decline is made. Response varies and may be absent. Discuss expectations with both the patient and the caregiver before consent.

WHAT TO EXPECT
Changes are gradual — do not judge the result in the first days
TODAY
A drip at the back of the throat, an odd taste, or mild congestion is normal, plus some arm soreness. The rest of the day can follow the usual routine.
FIRST WEEKS
Any change is gradual. Keep routines, sleep, and hydration steady, and note observations in the caregiver log rather than judging day to day.
BY FOLLOW-UP
Bring the log. Progress is measured against the baseline testing, not against memory of a good or bad day.
DO
•Keep the daily routine and sleep schedule steady
•Care partner: a one-line note in the log each day
•Continue all prescribed medications unchanged
•Drink water normally through the day
AVOID
•Blowing the nose forcefully for 4–6 hours
•Nasal sprays for 24 hours unless prescribed
•Alcohol for 72 hours
•Judging results in the first days
CALL US RIGHT AWAY IF
For an emergency, call 911 first
•Confusion suddenly worse than the usual baseline
•A fall, or any head injury
•Fever above 101 °F, or a nosebleed that does not stop
•New weakness or trouble speaking — call 911
